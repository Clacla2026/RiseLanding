History

Rise Diversity comes from a lived experience. A deeply human reality that is also shared by many young people from diverse backgrounds: the lack of access to professional opportunities, not because of a lack of talent or ambition, but because of a lack of social capital, networks, and genuine chances to be seen and heard.

Every year, bright and motivated young graduates step into the world with hope and determination, ready to transform their education, skills, and aspirations into meaningful careers. Yet for many young graduates and students seeking their first internship or job, the journey into the workforce can feel like navigating an invisible maze.

Too often, opportunities travel through informal networks, through connections, referrals, and circles that many young people from underrepresented communities have simply never had access to. As a result, even the most qualified and motivated individuals can find themselves on the margins of the very opportunities they worked so hard to reach.

This lived experience is not an isolated story. It is shared by countless young people who, despite their potential and dedication, face prolonged unemployment, underemployment, or precarious work after graduation. What should be a moment of promise, the transition from education to career, too often becomes a period of uncertainty, waiting, and questioning one's place in the professional world.

For many, particularly those from diverse backgrounds, this challenge is not the exception. It is the reality.

Goals

Our mission is simple, yet transformative: to build bridges where barriers exist. We believe that talent is everywhere, but opportunities are not always equally accessible. Rise Diversity is committed to strengthening the social capital of the next generation by connecting talented individuals from diverse backgrounds with the networks, mentors, and opportunities that can shape the course of a career and sometimes even a life.

Beyond the statistics are young people with dreams, skills, and immense potential. When talent finally meets opportunity, the impact goes far beyond individual success. It opens doors, strengthens communities, and moves society forward.

Rise Diversity stands on a simple but powerful belief: equal opportunity should not remain an ideal. It should be a reality.

Barriers

The invisible walls that hold talented people back from the opportunities they deserve.

Social Capital

This capital is the one that affects most minorities. It mainly relates to who knows you, your professional network, and your contacts. The fear of the unfamiliar also plays a role. Giving a chance to someone who is not known, who has no references or experience, is often perceived as a very big risk for companies.

An idea that needs to be demystified. Many visible minorities do not have the kinds of contacts or relationships they would like to have.

Cultural Capital

Another dimension of cultural capital helps explain these employment gaps. It includes elements such as language barriers, stereotypes, and different cultural codes. Diverse perspectives are not always shared, sometimes because individuals occupy positions that do not easily allow them to express their ideas, or because the environment does not always encourage the inclusion of cultural experiences different from the dominant one.

Recognizing these dynamics offers an opportunity to create workplaces where diverse perspectives are welcomed, heard, and valued.

Economic Capital

Last but not least is economic capital, which translates into no decent employment, no money, no development, and no material stability. It keeps many visible minorities at the doors of poverty and limits their development, unfortunately without having had the opportunity to put into practice or further develop the efforts and knowledge they have acquired academically, often while carrying debt on their shoulders.

Mental Capital

Not to mention mental health issues such as depression, or even delinquency, that can arise from the difficulty of finding employment in a field related to their studies or in a related area.

Process

  • For the process: We will partner with organizations that are willing to hire and give opportunities to our young professionals and graduates from visible minorities, allowing them to put into practice the skills they developed in the academic world or through previous professional experiences. This will offer them a space to grow, develop, and feel included in the work and in the organizations where they will contribute their skills.
  • From our young professionals: They will present their degree or diploma, share the skills they have or want to develop, and indicate the organizations they would like to work with. They will then be matched based on their needs and the needs of the organizations.

Why Trust Us?

Benefits for young professionals

  • We will be creating social capital for visible minorities.
  • Helping them grow in careers they would like to explore.
  • Allowing them to feel fulfilled in their professional paths.
  • Matching them to jobs in their fields where they can put their academic knowledge into practice.
  • Contributing to breaking employment gaps and poverty within this community.

Benefits for our Organization Partners

  • They will gain dedicated and motivated employees who are willing to learn and pursue a professional path.
  • They will benefit from employees who are qualified and reviewed.
  • They will welcome individuals who are genuinely interested in contributing to the organization.
  • They will engage employees who want to help elevate and strengthen the organization.
  • They will receive candidates who are referred and supported by us.
  • At the same time, this contributes to supporting EDI objectives within the organization.
